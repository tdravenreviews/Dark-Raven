REVIEWS / SURVIVAL · 2024

The Planet Crafter
A World Worth Growing

An empty planet, a handful of tools, and an almost unreasonable amount of potential. The Planet Crafter turns the lonely business of surviving on an alien world into one of the most satisfying slow-burn adventures in indie gaming.

REVIEW BY DARK RAVENPLAYED ON PC~ 30 HOURS

There's a special kind of satisfaction in looking out across a barren alien landscape and realizing that, somehow, you've made it better. The rocks are still there. The mountains haven't moved. But the skies are changing, the ground is turning green, and the lifeless wasteland you arrived on is slowly becoming something beautiful. The Planet Crafter understands that sometimes the best adventure isn't conquering a world. It's bringing one to life.

The short version

You arrive on an inhospitable planet with a simple mission: make it habitable for humanity. To get there, you'll scavenge resources, construct machines, establish a base, and gradually increase the planet's temperature, atmospheric pressure, and oxygen levels. What begins as a desperate struggle to stay alive slowly evolves into a sprawling terraforming project.

The gameplay loop is refreshingly straightforward. Gather materials, craft equipment, expand your operations, and unlock better technology. Each improvement contributes to your planet's transformation, and each milestone opens up new possibilities. There's no need to memorize elaborate combat systems or worry about hostile creatures jumping out of the bushes. The main challenge is learning what you need, finding it, and building the infrastructure to make the next stage possible.

What stands out

The terraforming is the star of the show. Watching a dead landscape develop an atmosphere, sprout vegetation, and eventually support increasingly complex forms of life is genuinely remarkable. Progress isn't merely a number on a screen; the world itself changes around you. A place that once looked utterly inhospitable gradually becomes somewhere you'd actually want to live.

Exploration adds another layer of delight. Scattered wrecks, abandoned structures, hidden caves, and unusual geological formations reward those willing to venture beyond their comfortable little base. Finding a new resource or stumbling across a tucked-away area can completely change your plans for the afternoon. The world has a wonderful sense of mystery, and those discoveries make gathering supplies feel less like running errands and more like an expedition.

There's also something wonderfully peaceful about the absence of conventional combat. The environment presents its own survival challenges, but the game largely lets you focus on building rather than fighting. Once your equipment improves and your base becomes self-sufficient, the experience settles into a satisfying rhythm. You can spend an evening redesigning your living quarters, expanding your machinery, or wandering into the wilderness simply because you're curious about what's over the next ridge.

And for anyone who enjoys building games with friends, cooperative multiplayer adds another reason to stick around. Dividing up resource gathering, exploring together, and watching your collective efforts transform the planet can make the journey feel even more rewarding.

Where it stumbles

Let's be honest: resource gathering can get repetitive. Early progression sometimes feels slow, and you'll occasionally find yourself making another trip for the same handful of materials. The crafting systems are accessible rather than particularly deep, and players looking for complex survival mechanics, challenging enemies, or a heavily scripted narrative may find the experience a little too simple.

There are stretches where the most sensible thing to do is wait for your machines to produce enough progress to unlock the next technology tier. The story and environmental discoveries provide some motivation, but the journey is primarily driven by your own curiosity and the satisfaction of watching the numbers climb. If that loop doesn't appeal to you, the game's central attraction may wear thin.

Final thoughts

The Planet Crafter succeeds because it makes progress feel tangible. Every machine, every expedition, and every carefully collected resource contributes to a world that looks and feels different from the one you started with. It transforms a familiar crafting formula into something quietly ambitious, giving you the rare pleasure of watching your hard work reshape an entire environment.

What I appreciate most is the game's sense of optimism. In a genre that often asks you to survive against a hostile world, this one invites you to heal one. There's something deeply satisfying about turning an empty rock into a living place, one small improvement at a time. It isn't the most mechanically sophisticated survival game out there, but it doesn't need to be. For players who love exploration, gradual progression, and building something that feels genuinely worthwhile, The Planet Crafter is an easy recommendation. Sometimes you don't need to save the galaxy. You just need to make one little corner of it bloom.

Spoiler note

This review covers the core terraforming mechanics and general exploration without revealing major discoveries or endgame story details.
