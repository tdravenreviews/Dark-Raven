REVIEWS / SURVIVAL · ASHLANDS UPDATE

Valheim
Ashlands Update

A friend charges into danger, another makes a home, and a third has already wandered off to explore. Valheim has room for all of them—and a world ready to test every plan.

REVIEW BY NERDY ERMINEASHLANDS UPDATE · 2024

Have you got an online friend who charges into battle without a second thought, armed with nothing more than a flint knife, and somehow comes out alive with one strip of health left? Or a friend who would rather build a home, do the chores, and cook? Maybe someone who vanishes the moment you turn your back, already exploring somewhere far away—or maybe you simply like your own company. Valheim has space for all of you.

The short version

Valheim blends survival, exploration, farming, and mining in a vast world of wild biomes. Play alone or with friends: tend a farm, defend your fort from destructive uninvited guests, or set out to discover what lies beyond the next ridge. There is always another place to explore and another reason to make it safely back home.

What stands out

Many survival games make combat the main way to restore your well-being. Valheim asks you to think differently before stepping into the wilderness—or even before deciding how to spend a quiet evening at home. Preparation and the things you eat matter, so your survival strategy starts before the fighting does.

Each biome has its own character and its own way of making exploration feel dangerous. A new place can bring a fresh kind of trouble, or a new phobia to take back to your warm, safe base. And just when you start to relax, an unwelcome visitor from nearby—or far away—may show up at your door.

For all its danger, Valheim can be beautiful. The world’s scenery and instrumental music give each biome its own mood, and together they make the wilderness feel memorable. Boss fights and everyday battles can take on a cinematic quality, too. After a close call or a long trip home with valuable loot, sitting down to eat and rest at your base feels earned. The game may even remind you to stand up, stretch, or grab a snack before heading back out.

Where it stumbles

Valheim's progression has a familiar pattern: reach a new biome or defeat a major boss, then mine, explore, and gather what you need to craft or upgrade the next thing. That cycle can start to feel like a chore. New rewards help soften the repetition, whether they are useful items or simply the view from somewhere you have never been.

The game also lets you shape your fighter by investing in the skills that suit your preferred role. That feels good early on, but the rising difficulty can push you to adapt and leave that role behind to survive and keep progressing.

Combat is unforgiving, and Valheim can make the bigger number feel like the only safe choice. You may have to commit to your current weapon to deal enough damage, or abandon it and build skill with a stronger one. That pressure makes role-playing harder, especially solo, and can steer you toward becoming an all-purpose fighter by the end.

Final thoughts

Valheim delivers challenging battles in an enchanting world, whether you play solo or with friends. Its generated maps feel vast and beautiful, but also dangerous. The daily rhythm of building, gathering, preparing, and venturing out gives the game a strong sense of place—even when the work begins to repeat.

For its memorable world and satisfying, demanding survival gameplay, I give Valheim's Ashlands update an 8.6/10.
